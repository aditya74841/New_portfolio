He had been in the city for only a few months.

It was a new city.
No friends. No one he could call his own.

His family was far away, and the room he lived in was a home, but it wasn't really his.

Going to work in the morning, coming back in the evening, and then sitting alone in that same room—slowly, his life had become nothing more than this routine.

Every month, the rent due date came and reminded him that he had to give away a part of his income just to live in this city.

Sometimes the rent worried him.
Sometimes the electricity bill.

Sometimes he worried about his job.
Sometimes about his future.

Slowly, he started becoming frustrated with his life.

One day, he went downstairs to pay the rent.

The landlord was sitting outside.

He had a big house of his own.

His family was sitting nearby.

The children were laughing. His wife was talking to him. A few friends had also come over.

The landlord had a car.

He had money.

A house.

A family.

Friends.

And on top of all that, he received rent every month from the ten apartments in the building.

The tenant stood there for a while, watching him.

Then he quietly went back to his room.

He closed the door and sat on his bed.

One thought kept running through his mind—

“What do I even have in my life?”

He thought about the landlord.

“His life is completely sorted.”

“He has a house.”

“A family.”

“Money.”

“Friends.”

“And every month, he gets rent too.”

Then he looked at his own life.

“I am alone here.”

“My family is far away.”

“I don't have my own house.”

“I don't have any friends.”

“And on top of everything, I have to worry about paying rent every month.”

His heart felt even heavier.

He thought—

“Why does everyone else seem to have everything while I have nothing?”

That night, he couldn't sleep for a long time.

The next morning, when he came downstairs, he heard the landlord talking to someone on the phone.

There was stress in his voice.

“Can't the EMI date be extended?”

A little later, he hung up.

Just then, another tenant came and said that he wouldn't be able to pay the rent that month.

The landlord stayed silent for a moment.

Then someone came and told him that a pipe had burst in one of the upstairs apartments.

There would be another repair expense.

A little later, his son got upset with him over something and walked away.

The landlord remained sitting in his chair.

The tiredness on his face was clearly visible.

The man whom the tenant had thought was completely free from worries now looked worried himself.

The tenant stood there, watching everything.

For the first time, he realized—

He had never really known the whole story behind the life he had been judging from the outside.

The man he thought only had money also had financial worries.

The man he thought was happy because he had a family also had problems within his family.

The man who owned multiple houses also carried the responsibility of maintaining them.

He quietly went back to his room.

But this time, he didn't feel the same restlessness he had felt the day before.

His problems hadn't disappeared.

He still had to pay the rent.

He still worried about his job.

His family was still far away.

He was still alone.

But something inside him had changed.

He was no longer comparing his life with the landlord's.

He had understood something—

Everyone is fighting their own battle.

For some, it's money.

For some, it's family.

For some, it's loneliness.

For others, it's the future.

And sometimes, the person we look at and think—

“Their life must be so easy.”

—may be looking at someone else and thinking exactly the same thing.

That day, for the first time, the tenant stopped looking at his life through the eyes of complaint and looked at it with a little more peace.

He told himself—

“My life is difficult, but I'm not the only one whose life is difficult.”

And perhaps, that realization was enough.

Because his problems hadn't ended that day.

Only the way he looked at them had changed.

We often see only the part of someone else's life that is visible from the outside.

We see their money, their house, their family, their success.

But we don't see their sleepless nights, their fears, their responsibilities, or their struggles.

Maybe the person you're looking at today and thinking—

“I wish my life was like theirs.”

—is sitting alone somewhere tonight thinking the same thing about someone else.

Because everyone has something we wish we had.

And everyone also has something they struggle with that we cannot see.

We know our entire story,
but we see only a glimpse of someone else's.

So perhaps, before comparing your life with someone else's, it's worth pausing for a moment.

Because—

Everyone has their own battle.

The only difference is that not every battle is visible.
